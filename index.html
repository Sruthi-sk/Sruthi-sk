Probabilistic Calibration Is a Trainable Capability in Language Models

MARS 4.0

Ask a model for a random weekday and it says Wednesday 80% of the time. We show probabilistic calibration is a trainable capability rather than an emergent side-effect: trie-based soft targets cut KL by an order of magnitude on held-out distribution families and transfer to natural language (Wednesday bias 80%→40%) with minimal capability cost — with relevance to alignment and white-box monitoring.

Reasoning LLMs Can Subvert Intervention-Based Faithfulness Checks

📍 Report · 2nd Place, Impact First Research Fellowship

As tasks get harder, non-thinking and distilled models increasingly follow injected incorrect reasoning — while some thinking models (Qwen3) actively revise it, breaking the difficulty-consistency assumption behind CoT monitoring. We design experiments that scale reasoning-task difficulty and show that intervention-based faithfulness checks can be systematically subverted, raising questions about the reliability of CoT monitorability as a safety guarantee.

Runaway-Optimiser-Like Failure Modes on Bio & Economically Aligned Benchmarks

📍 LessWrong · arXiv · bioBLUE

In long-horizon control environments, LLMs behave competently for many steps, then drift in structured ways: collapsing multi-objective trade-offs into single-objective maximisation, ignoring homeostatic targets, and falling into self-imitative loops — well before the context window fills. Our paper evaluates this under biologically and economically inspired constraints (homeostasis, sustainability, multi-objective trade-offs), surfacing systematic runaway-optimiser-like failures and highlighting alignment risks in long-running scenarios.

Interpreting the Distributional Successor Measure as a Model of the Hippocampus

📍 MSc Thesis · University College London · Distinction

This thesis investigates the Distributional Successor Measure (DSM) as a computational model of the hippocampus. I train a generative, particle-based distributional reinforcement-learning model using an adversarial MMD loss to capture future-state distributions in spatial tasks, with structured inputs inspired by hippocampal place cells. Probing the learned representations reveals task- and environment-specific spatial structure, with latent components that linearly decode present and future locations — mirroring neural and behavioural activity. Benchmarked against the classic Successor Representation, the DSM shows ~15% greater representational stability under remapping, indicating improved robustness to environmental perturbation.

Can LLMs Metacognitively Monitor & Control Their Own Internals?

📍 MARS 4.0 · ongoing

Testing whether LLMs can deliberately monitor and control their own output distributions — early results show models can modulate output entropy on instruction, but with systematic position bias (option A). Toward white-box monitoring: if a model can control its uncertainty, when can you trust its reports of it?

Honeypots for Scheming & Evaluation-Awareness

📍 Athena

Built honeypot environments plus linear probes and activation steering to test whether evaluation-awareness causally ablates scheming — probing when a model's sense that it is "being tested" drives its propensity to scheme, and whether steering that representation changes behaviour.
