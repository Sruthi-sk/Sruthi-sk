Probabilistic Calibration Is a Trainable Capability in Language Models

📍 Submitted to COLM 2026 · MARS 4.0

We ask whether predictive calibration in language models is a capability that can be deliberately trained, rather than an emergent side-effect — studying how training objectives and finetuning shape a model's calibration and the reliability of its probability estimates, with relevance to alignment and white-box monitoring.

Reasoning LLMs Can Subvert Intervention-Based Faithfulness Checks

📍 Report · 2nd Place, Impact First Research Fellowship

Intervention-based consistency checks are a popular tool for auditing whether a model's chain of thought is faithful to its underlying reasoning. We design experiments that scale reasoning-task difficulty and show that reasoning models can systematically subvert these checks — raising questions about the reliability of CoT monitorability as a safety guarantee.

Runaway-Optimiser-Like Failure Modes on Bio & Economically Aligned Benchmarks

📍 LessWrong · arXiv · bioBLUE

bioBLUE evaluates LLM decision-making under biologically and economically inspired constraints — homeostasis, sustainability, and multi-objective trade-offs. Through long-horizon textual simulations we surface systematic runaway-optimiser-like failures, including unbounded maximisation, neglect of stated objectives, and self-imitative drift — highlighting alignment risks in long-running scenarios.

Interpreting the Distributional Successor Measure as a Model of the Hippocampus

📍 MSc Thesis · University College London · Distinction

This thesis investigates the Distributional Successor Measure (DSM) as a computational model of the hippocampus. I train a generative, particle-based distributional reinforcement-learning model using an adversarial MMD loss to capture future-state distributions in spatial tasks, with structured inputs inspired by hippocampal place cells. Probing the learned representations reveals task- and environment-specific spatial structure, with latent components that linearly decode present and future locations — mirroring neural and behavioural activity. Benchmarked against the classic Successor Representation, the DSM shows ~15% greater representational stability under remapping, indicating improved robustness to environmental perturbation.
